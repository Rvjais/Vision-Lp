UNDERSTANDING CATARACTS
What Is a Cataract?
A cataract is a condition in which the natural lens of the eye becomes cloudy, affecting the ability to see clearly.

It commonly develops with age but can also occur due to eye injuries, certain medical conditions, medications or other factors.

As cataracts progress, patients may experience blurred vision, increased sensitivity to light, glare and difficulty performing everyday activities.

Cataract surgery involves removing the cloudy natural lens and replacing it with an artificial intraocular lens (IOL) to help improve vision.

Your ophthalmologist will assess the stage of cataract development and recommend the appropriate treatment based on your eye health.

CATARACT SYMPTOMS & WARNING SIGNS
Are You Experiencing Any of These Vision Problems?
Recognising cataract symptoms can help you understand when to seek an eye examination.

Blurred or Cloudy Vision
Objects may appear unclear, hazy or less sharp than before, even when using glasses.

Difficulty Seeing at Night
You may experience difficulty driving or navigating in low-light conditions.

Increased Glare & Light Sensitivity
Bright sunlight, vehicle headlights and other light sources may cause discomfort or glare.

Halos Around Lights
You may notice rings or halos around lights, particularly at night.

Frequent Changes in Glasses Prescription
Your spectacle prescription may change more frequently as cataracts develop.

Faded or Yellowish Colours
Colours may appear dull, less vibrant or slightly yellow compared to before.

Important: These symptoms may also occur due to other eye conditions. A comprehensive eye examination is necessary to identify the underlying cause.

CATARACT SURGERY & LENS OPTIONS
Understanding Your Intraocular Lens (IOL) Choices
During cataract surgery, the cloudy natural lens is replaced with an artificial intraocular lens (IOL).

Different lens options may be available depending on your eye measurements, visual requirements and clinical suitability.

Monofocal IOL
Designed to provide clear vision at one primary focal distance, commonly distance vision. Glasses may still be required for other activities, such as reading.

Multifocal IOL
Designed to provide vision at multiple distances and may reduce dependence on glasses for selected patients.

Extended Depth of Focus (EDOF) IOL
Designed to provide an extended range of vision, particularly for distance and intermediate activities, depending on the lens design.

Toric IOL
Designed to correct corneal astigmatism in suitable patients undergoing cataract surgery.

Which IOL Is Right for You?

The choice of lens depends on your corneal measurements, retinal health, lifestyle, visual expectations and other clinical factors.

Your ophthalmologist will explain the available options, potential benefits and limitations before recommending a suitable lens.

CATARACT SURGERY CONCERNS
Have Questions About Cataract Surgery?
Understanding the procedure, recovery and possible risks can help you make an informed decision.

Is Cataract Surgery Safe?
Cataract surgery is a commonly performed procedure. Like all surgeries, it carries potential risks and complications. Your ophthalmologist will explain the benefits, risks and precautions relevant to your eye condition.

Will I Experience Pain During Surgery?
Cataract surgery is generally performed using local anaesthesia, commonly with anaesthetic eye drops. Some patients may experience mild pressure or discomfort during the procedure.

How Long Does Cataract Surgery Take?
The surgical procedure is generally completed within a relatively short period. The exact duration depends on the complexity of the cataract and individual surgical requirements.

Will I Need Glasses After Cataract Surgery?
Your need for glasses depends on the intraocular lens selected, your eye condition and the type of vision correction planned. Some patients may still require glasses for certain activities.

Can Cataracts Develop Again After Surgery?
The removed natural lens does not develop another cataract. However, some patients may develop posterior capsule opacification (PCO), sometimes called a secondary cataract. It can often be treated using a YAG laser procedure.

How Long Does Recovery Take?
Recovery varies between individuals. Your ophthalmologist will provide personalised instructions regarding eye care, medication, activity restrictions and follow-up visits.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Cataract Surgery FAQs
1. What is cataract surgery?
Cataract surgery is a procedure in which the cloudy natural lens of the eye is removed and replaced with an artificial intraocular lens (IOL) to help improve vision.

2. What are the early symptoms of cataracts?
Common symptoms include blurred vision, glare, difficulty seeing at night, faded colours, halos around lights and frequent changes in spectacle prescriptions.

3. When should I get cataract surgery?
Cataract surgery may be recommended when reduced vision begins affecting your everyday activities and quality of life. Your ophthalmologist will determine the appropriate timing after examining your eyes.

4. Is cataract surgery painful?
Cataract surgery is generally performed using local anaesthesia, often with anaesthetic eye drops. Patients may experience mild pressure or temporary discomfort.

5. What is phacoemulsification cataract surgery?
Phacoemulsification is a surgical technique that uses ultrasound energy to break the cloudy natural lens into smaller fragments before removing it and implanting an artificial lens.

6. What are the different types of lenses used in cataract surgery?
Common IOL options include monofocal, multifocal, extended depth of focus (EDOF) and toric lenses. The appropriate lens depends on your eye condition and visual requirements.

7. Can cataracts be treated without surgery?
Surgery is currently the established treatment for visually significant cataracts. Eye drops, medication or spectacles cannot remove an existing cataract. Your ophthalmologist can advise when surgery is appropriate.

8. Can cataracts come back after surgery?
The original cataract does not return because the cloudy natural lens has been removed. However, posterior capsule opacification (PCO) can develop in some patients and may be treated using a YAG laser.

9. How long does it take to recover after cataract surgery?
Recovery varies depending on individual eye health and the surgical procedure. Your ophthalmologist will provide specific guidance regarding healing, medication and follow-up.

10. Can both eyes undergo cataract surgery at the same time?
The treatment plan depends on your eye condition and clinical requirements. Your ophthalmologist will determine the appropriate timing for surgery in each eye.

11. Will I still need glasses after cataract surgery?
Some patients may continue to need glasses for reading, distance or specific activities. The requirement depends on the IOL selected and individual visual needs.

12. Is cataract surgery suitable for diabetic patients?
People with diabetes may undergo cataract surgery after an appropriate eye evaluation. Retinal health and diabetic eye conditions need to be assessed before surgical planning.

13. What precautions should I take after cataract surgery?
Patients are generally advised to follow prescribed eye-drop schedules, avoid rubbing or pressing the operated eye and follow their ophthalmologist's instructions regarding bathing, physical activity and follow-up examinations.

14. How do I choose the right cataract surgeon and hospital?
Consider the ophthalmologist's qualifications, experience, diagnostic facilities, surgical infrastructure, available lens options and the quality of pre- and post-operative care.
